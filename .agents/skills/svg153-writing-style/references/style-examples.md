# Style evidence and editing contrasts

These short user-authored excerpts were supplied in conversation. They illustrate particular signals, not a complete profile for every channel. Proposed revisions below are assistant-authored teaching examples, not user-approved writing. Topics, names and private operational details are omitted. Do not import example facts into a new draft.

## Close-contact message

User-authored excerpt: «Si no pues hacemos post-partido y comentamos después jaja».

Signal: a concrete alternative and spontaneous humor in an informal exchange. It does not establish an emoji or humor requirement for professional recipients.

Proposed edit: «Si no se puede, hacemos post-partido y lo comentamos después jaja».

Avoid: turning a practical question into a formal request for institutional authorization, or stacking smileys to simulate familiarity.

## Technical conversation

User-authored excerpt: «Pero es verdad que el otro día lancé un prompt».

Signal: a conversational transition into a concrete experience. Preserve the example and qualification when relevant; remove repeated starts around it.

Proposed editing pattern: context, concrete observation, practical implication. Do not invent the experience, amounts or conclusions.

## Organizing a dictated message

User-authored instruction: «como te he contado las ideas un poco desestructuradas [...] estructúramelo un poco».

Signal: preserving voice permits changing order. Group related ideas and remove abandoned starts instead of reproducing the transcript's sequence.

Proposed editing pattern: purpose first, necessary explanation next, request or alternative last. Keep substantive uncertainty; remove empty hesitation.

## Professional correspondence

No approved complete professional-email exemplar is bundled here. Retrieve or request one when needed. Use concise context, a direct request and a natural closing as conservative defaults, not as a verified reconstruction of the author's email style.

## Selecting and retaining evidence

A designated example takes precedence. Otherwise match channel, recipient relationship and purpose, then prefer recent evidence. A casual recent dictation must not override a relevant professional sample. Keep explicit preferences separate from inferred patterns. Do not store private correspondence or names in this public skill. Add future approved examples only with clear provenance and an appropriate disclosure scope; never silently promote an assistant draft to approved evidence.
